The Mental Model

The claims journey starts when something unexpected happens and ends when the customer has been paid and had a chance to rate the experience.

The three strategic corridors

Every step of the claims journey belongs to one of these corridors — two customer-facing, one operational.

EVA

Report claim, payment and settlement. Covers the start and end of every claim: describing what happened, submitting the report, and — after any repair or treatment — registering expenses and receiving compensation.

Frank

Search, booking, repair and treatment. Used only when the claim requires a repair (e.g. vehicle) or treatment (e.g. injury) — customers who receive an immediate cash settlement skip Frank entirely.

Sofie

Manual claims handling. Not a single application — the operational corridor where claims requiring human assessment (Yellow, and some Red outcomes) are handled, at any point across the journey.

Sofie

Sofie — Manual Claims Handling

Sofie isn't a single application — it's the strategic corridor for manual claims handling. It supports claims that require assessment and work by a claims handler, at any point in the journey.

Claims Communication

Runs across the full journey, connecting the customer with Gjensidige and, where relevant, third parties — regardless of which corridor or step the claim is in.

Who uses what

  • Customer-facing = the customer uses it directly.
  • Handler systems = the claims handler uses it (Sofie).
  • Backend systems = neither the customer nor the handler typically interacts with directly — they provide data, automation, storage and integrations.
